Methodology

How we measure trade dependencies

This atlas extends the five-criteria methodology of Mejean and Rousseaux (2024) to a large cross-section of economies, and introduces reverse dependency indicators measuring the leverage an economy exercises over others.

1. Why measure trade dependencies?

Global value chains (GVCs) have undergone significant hyper-globalisation over the past three decades, leading to highly dispersed geographical production. This fragmentation has increased trade gains but also heightened exposure to supply shocks: disruptions at any concentrated GVC node can cascade downstream through industries and consumers (di Giovanni et al., 2020; Bonadio et al., 2021).

The Covid-19 pandemic and the Russian invasion of Ukraine demonstrated how heavily economies rely on a few concentrated suppliers for critical goods — pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, rare earths, chemicals. Identifying these vulnerabilities is a prerequisite for designing effective resilience policies. The current context of US tariffs on Europe adds further urgency: it is essential to identify not only our dependencies on third countries but also the leverage we hold over them.

2. Identifying import dependencies

A product is classified as a critical import dependency of country j if it simultaneously satisfies all relevant criteria. We implement three nested lists.

2.1 European Commission criteria (List 1: EC)

The European Commission's bottom-up approach identifies a product k as a potential vulnerability for country j if it meets three simultaneous criteria:

EC Criterion 1
Import concentration
HHI(M_jk) > 0.4
The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index of extra-zone import sources for product k exceeds 0.4, indicating high supplier concentration.
EC Criterion 2
Extra-zone import significance
M_extra_jk / M_total_jk > 0.5
Extra-zone imports account for more than 50% of total imports of product k by country j.
EC Criterion 3
Import-to-export substitutability
M_extra_jk / X_jk > 1
Extra-zone imports of k exceed country j's exports of k, ruling out easy domestic substitution via export redirection.

2.2 Absorption criterion (List 2: EC + Absorption)

Mejean and Rousseaux (2024) add a fourth criterion measuring domestic absorption: the degree to which extra-zone imports fill domestic demand net of intra-zone flows.

MR Criterion 4
Domestic absorption constraint
M_extra_jk / Absorption_jk > 0.5
Absorption = Production_j + M_total_jk − X_jk. Extra-zone imports cover more than 50% of effective domestic demand, implying limited scope for domestic substitution. Requires Prodcom (EU27 + UK) or equivalent production data.

2.3 Stickiness criterion (List 3: EC + Absorption + Stickiness)

A fifth criterion captures supplier stickiness — the difficulty of switching suppliers post-shock — using the relationship stickiness metric of Martin, Mejean, and Parenti (2024).

MR Criterion 5
Supplier stickiness
rs_k > Q75(rs)
The product's relationship stickiness score (Martin et al., 2024) is in the top quartile of the HS6 distribution across 5,567 products, indicating very low substitutability between suppliers ex-post.

3. Reverse dependency indicators

For each source economy s, we compute the mirror image: the set of products for which s is a critical supplier to other economies j. A product k is a reverse dependency of s on j if it simultaneously satisfies:

Reverse Criterion 1
Concentrated market + source presence
HHI(M_jk) > 0.4 AND s_share > 0
The import market for k in j is concentrated, and s is an active supplier in that market.
Reverse Criterion 2
Source dominance in j's imports
X_s→j,k / M_jk > 0.5
Economy s supplies more than half of j's imports of product k.
Reverse Criterion 3
Export power
X_s→j,k / X_jk > 1
Economy s's exports of k to j exceed j's own total exports of k, ruling out easy redirection of domestic production.

4. Aggregate indicators

4.1 Vulnerability Index (VI)

For economy j, the Vulnerability Index aggregates critical import values weighted by the product's position in global value chains (upstreamness from Antràs and Chor, 2022), normalised by GDP:

VI_j = Σ_k ( M_extra_jk × upstreamness_k ) / GDP_j
// Extra-zone import value in EUR 2019 constant, upstreamness ≥ 1
// GDP in EUR billion (World Bank + Eurostat for EU27)

Upstreamness ≈ 1 indicates a final good; upstreamness ≫ 1 indicates an upstream intermediate input whose disruption propagates through many downstream sectors.

4.2 Leverage Index (LI)

For source economy s, the Leverage Index aggregates the critical export values it supplies to each importing economy j, weighted by upstreamness and normalised by j's GDP:

LI_sj = Σ_k ( X_s→j,k × upstreamness_k ) / GDP_j
// Measures the share of j's GDP controlled by s via critical exports

The total leverage of s over the world is LI_s = Σ_j LI_sj / GDP_s.

4.3 Dependency Balance Score (DBS)

DBS_j = VI_j / LI_j
// DBS < 1 → economy j exports more leverage than it imports (net powerful)
// DBS > 1 → economy j is more vulnerable than it is powerful (net vulnerable)
// DBS ≈ 1 → balanced

The DBS is the main indicator on the atlas homepage map. Economies coloured blue (DBS < 1) hold net leverage over others; economies coloured red (DBS > 1) are net importers of critical risk.

6. Scope and limitations

The absorption criterion (criterion 4) requires domestic production data and is therefore only available for EU27 member states and the United Kingdom (Prodcom). For all other economies (US, China, Japan, etc.), only the three EC criteria are applied, and stickiness is computed directly on EC products without the absorption filter.
  • All trade values are in EUR 2019 constant prices, deflated using the EU27 PPI from Eurostat.
  • The "extra-zone" definition varies by country: for EU member states it means non-EU27; for all other economies it means rest of world.
  • The pooled 2015–2019 period is used as the baseline (pre-shock), reflecting persistent structural dependencies rather than annual fluctuations.
  • Production data for countries outside EU/UK would significantly improve coverage. If you have production data for additional countries, please get in touch.
